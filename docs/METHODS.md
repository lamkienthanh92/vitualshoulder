# Methods (v0.3)

## Frame
Origin at the humeral head centre; x = subject's left (right shoulder lies at x < 0), y = up, z = anterior.

## Landmarks (computed from the meshes)
- Humeral head: sphere fitted to the proximal humerus (robust least squares), R = 22.9 mm.
- Glenoid: scapular vertices near the head centre whose normals face it, largest connected cluster; centre, normal, superior/inferior poles. Height 32 mm, width 26 mm in this model.
- LHBT centreline: geodesic distance along the biceps long-head mesh from the supraglenoid tubercle, binned every 2 mm.
- Muscle footprints: muscle vertices within 2.5 mm of bone surface samples.

## Kinematics
- Humerus orientation relative to thorax = R_k(plane, elevation) · R_y(axial) · R_align. R_align makes the head-centre-to-epicondyle axis vertical at 0° elevation; the transepicondylar axis then lies in the coronal plane (0° axial rotation).
- Scapular upward rotation (optional): theta = max(0, elevation − 30) / 3, about the scapular-plane normal through the clavicle's lateral end (AC point). Clavicle fixed. GH angle shown as elevation − theta (approximation).

## Muscles
Linear blend skinning between scapula, clavicle and humerus. Weights from inverse cubed distance to each bone surface (rotator cuff: scapula/humerus smoothstep ratio). Biceps long-head belly follows the humerus.

## LHBT path
Start: supraglenoid tubercle (scapula). End: groove entry (humerus). Between them the tendon is the shortest path around a sphere (R = 25.8 mm, centre = head centre); then it follows the groove centreline rigidly with the humerus. Length at arm-at-side (plane 30°, no rotation) = 63.4 mm; model estimate −27% at 90° abduction with scapular rhythm. Not validated against specimens or imaging.

## Checks done
Internal only: shaft vertical at 0°; elbow direction for abduction/flexion; glenoid normal rises with scapular rotation; software-rendered views of several poses. No comparison with in-vivo or cadaveric kinematics.

## SCR tab (v0.2)
- Anchors: glenoid side = `supraglenoid` landmark (moves with the scapula); humeral side = `gt` landmark, the centroid of supraspinatus mesh vertices within 2.5 mm of the humerus (moves with the humerus).
- Graft path: shortest path from the glenoid anchor to the humeral anchor wrapped over the humeral head sphere (R = head radius, same `wrapPath` as the LHBT); also the straight distance between anchors. Ribbon width is along the scapular-plane normal.
- Curves and the tension table use glenohumeral motion only (scapulohumeral rhythm off), in the plane and axial rotation currently selected (table: scapular plane 30 deg, rotation 0).
- Delta versus fixation = L(angle) - L(fixation angle); positive = anchors further apart than at fixation (graft taut), assuming an inextensible graft.
- Result in this single model (scapular plane, rotation 0): draped length 44.1 mm at 10 deg and 36.4 mm at 30 deg (slope -0.39 mm/deg); straight distance 39.3 and 33.8 mm (slope -0.28 mm/deg). Mihata 2016 reported 46 and 41 mm (-0.25 mm/deg) from cadaveric measurements. Not a validation.
- A separate analysis of six real scapula/humerus pairs (Zenodo 14590062, CC-BY) gave straight-line slopes of -0.11 to -0.19 mm/deg (mean -0.13, SD 0.03) with the dataset GT landmark as humeral anchor.

## DICOM tab (v0.3)
- Reader: explicit/implicit VR little endian, 8/16-bit, rescale slope/intercept; series grouped by SeriesInstanceUID and sorted along the slice normal. Multiplanar views are resampled (nearest voxel) on patient LPS axes (x left, y posterior, z superior).
- Landmarks (mm, patient coordinates): SG superior glenoid rim, GT medial edge of the supraspinatus footprint, HH humeral head centre, SH centre of the shaft at least 5 cm below the head, GA and GP anterior and posterior glenoid rim on one axial slice; optional AC and HT (head apex) for the acromiohumeral distance and the head radius.
- Frame: n = unit(GA - GP), the scapular-plane normal (glenoid anteroposterior axis); down = -z projected perpendicular to n; lateral = n x down, signed toward HH. Pose abduction phi0 = atan2(s.lat, s.down), s = unit(SH - HH).
- Simulation: GT is rotated about the shaft axis through HH until it lies in the scapular plane (neutral rotation; optional extra rotation psi, or keep the scan rotation), then about the axis (down x lat) by (phi - phi0). Length = |SG - GT'|; with HT the draped length over the head sphere (R = |HH - HT|) is also given. Delta versus fixation = L(phi) - L(phi_fix); inextensible graft assumed.
- Check: the same maths reproduces, to 0.1 mm, the numbers of an independent Python implementation on six real scapula/humerus pairs (Zenodo 14590062, CC-BY) and on a synthetic CT built from one of them read through the DICOM tab (L = 48.4, 47.8, 45.6, 39.7 mm at 0, 10, 30, 60 degrees).
- Limits: nearest-neighbour resampling, uniform slice spacing assumed, no gantry tilt handling, no compressed transfer syntaxes. Landmark placement error of 2 mm moves the length by about the same amount. No validation on patients.

## v0.4 additions
- **Head superior migration:** the humeral head centre is moved along the glenoid superior axis by a user-set distance (0–8 mm); presets from Mihata 2012/2017 (see `EVIDENCE.md`).
- **Anchor grids:** candidate anchors are the surface vertices of the scapular superior neck (3 mm grid, ±12 mm anteroposterior, up to 14 mm medial, upward-facing) and of the greater tuberosity top (3 mm grid around the supraspinatus footprint centroid, articular shell excluded). The graft length is the shortest path wrapped over the head sphere (R = 22.9 mm) between the two anchors.
- **Isometry measure:** maximum absolute change of graft length for GH abduction 0, 10, …, 60° relative to the fixation angle, at the chosen plane and rotation, without scapular rhythm.
- **Patient 3D view:** DICOM patient coordinates (LPS) map to the scene as (x, z, −y) with the head centre (HH) at the origin; left shoulders are mirrored in x (triangle winding swapped for STL). STL files are read as binary or ASCII; RAS files are converted to LPS.
- **Checks done:** default graft lengths identical to v0.3; flags, print sheet, anchor grids (44 glenoid, 27 humeral candidates), STL parsing and patient view run without script errors in a simulated browser. Not tested on real CT/MRI series or on a physical browser GPU.

## STL-only mode (v0.4.1)
For a scapula + humerus STL pair of one case (file names with Humerus/Scapula and _L_ or _R_):
1. Head centre and radius: robust (Tukey-weighted) sphere fit to the proximal 28 mm of the humerus, proximal end = the end nearer the scapula.
2. Glenoid: scapular surface 8 mm inside to 16 mm outside the head radius whose normal faces the head centre (cos > 0.7); largest connected cluster. Superior axis = principal axis of the cluster, signed away from the vertex farthest from the glenoid (inferior angle). Superior pole = SG; anterior/posterior rim = GA/GP along the axis perpendicular to the glenoid normal and the superior axis (anterior from the side label and handedness).
3. Greater tuberosity (GT): the template humerus (mirrored for left) is registered to the patient humerus: head centres coincident, shafts aligned, rotation about the shaft chosen by a 6° search on the median nearest-surface distance, then 12 ICP iterations with similarity transform (trimmed at 80%). The template footprint point is carried over and snapped to the nearest non-articular vertex.
4. Canonical frame: origin at the head centre, x = patient left, y = posterior, z = glenoid superior axis; this is the frame the DICOM simulation already expects.
Test on four cases (three left, one right): head fit 80–89% inliers, glenoid height 38–43 mm, template-to-bone median error 0.7–1.1 mm. Not compared against manual landmarks.

## LHBT techniques (v0.4.2)
Each technique is a list of bundles; a bundle is a glenoid anchor (scapula-fixed) and a humeral anchor (humerus-fixed) joined by the shortest path over the head sphere. Box = two bundles (SG–anterior GT, posterior glenoid rim–posterior GT), Snake = two spans sharing the posterosuperior glenoid anchor (anterior GT → glenoid → posterior GT), Chinese way = SG to the middle of the GT, biceps loop = posterosuperior glenoid rim to posterior GT (fixation 45°), in situ = SG to the supraspinatus footprint centroid. Anchor positions are the app's reading of the published descriptions (see `EVIDENCE.md`). For several bundles the length shown is the sum.

## Batch and validation (STL)
"Run all cases" runs the automatic analysis on every complete scapula/humerus pair and tabulates head radius, glenoid height and width, template fit error, abduction at scan, graft length at 0, 10, 30, 60° and the 10→30° slope. Landmarks moved by hand are stored per case; the comparison CSV lists auto vs edited coordinates and the distance per landmark with mean and maximum per landmark. These distances measure disagreement between the automatic and the manually edited landmark of the same user, not accuracy against a reference standard.

## Decision-support variables (v0.5)
- **Fixation-angle matrix:** for fixation at 0, 10, 30, 45, 60° the graft length at fixation and the change of length at 0, 30, 60, 90° (mm). Positive = the graft must be longer than at fixation (tight), negative = slack. Computed for the template model (current plane and rotation) and for each patient (STL or DICOM landmarks).
- **Rotation sensitivity:** length at the fixation angle for humeral rotation −40…+40° relative to the current rotation.
- **Head elevation sensitivity (model):** change of required length per 1 mm of superior head translation.
- **Bone clearance (STL pairs):** about 1200 sampled humeral points within 75 mm of the head centre are rotated as in the graft simulation (about the shaft by the neutral-rotation offset plus the chosen rotation, then about the glenoid anteroposterior axis by abduction minus abduction at scan) and the minimum distance to scapular roof vertices is taken, every 10° from 0 to 120°. At the scan pose without rotation the same distance is reported as the clearance at scan. In four test cases: 5.2–10.5 mm at scan, minimum 3.8–6.8 mm at 60–110°. No external reference was available.
- **Clinical profile:** three optional inputs (Goutallier, Hamada, LHB tear %) produce flags using only thresholds stated in the cited papers.

## Display (v0.5)
- The user interface is English only; decimals use a point; exported CSV and HTML plans are English.
- The long head of biceps tendon is drawn as a flattened band (about 6 mm wide, 2.3 mm thick) whose flat side lies against the bone surface, tapering where it joins the muscle belly (shown by default). The groove centreline is smoothed. The tendon of the SCR graft (LHBT techniques) uses the same flattened profile with the chosen width.
- The default and "Fit to view" camera is centred on the shoulder region (scapula, clavicle, proximal humerus), not on the humeral shaft.

## Plan comparison (v0.6)
Up to eight plans (or all seven techniques at default settings) are stored as snapshots and scored on the template model with glenohumeral motion only: graft length at fixation; change of length at 0, 30, 60 and 90° (positive = taut, negative = slack); peak taut strain over 0–60°; excursion over 0–90°; maximum change over 0–60°; slope between 10 and 30° and its ratio to the −0.25 mm/° of Mihata 2016; flag counts; and the literature efficacy sentence for the technique. The comparison table and a chart of length change against abduction (greyscale dashes) are included in the printed plan and a CSV can be saved.

## Own cadaver reference (v0.6.1)
Width and thickness of the long head of biceps tendon (mean ± SD: 6.2 ± 1.8 mm and 3.4 ± 0.7 mm; 4 of 10 tendons narrower than 5 mm) and superior capsule thickness (anterior 2.7 ± 0.9 mm, posterior 1.6 ± 0.7 mm), n = 10 shoulders from 5 cadavers. Used for: the cross-section of the tendon and LHBT graft drawn in 3D (width-to-thickness ratio 0.56), the graft-to-capsule thickness ratio in flags and in the plan comparison, and the LHB tendon range flag. Tendon footprint and tendon-portion dimensions in the same table are not used yet (definition to be confirmed).

## Explore dashboard (v0.7)
All outputs come from the same glenohumeral-only model as the plan comparison. Length change at abduction e is L(e) − L(fixation); peak taut strain is the largest positive change over 0–60° divided by the length at fixation; slack at 60° is L(fixation) − L(60°). The trade-off chart plots, for each fixation angle f, L(0°) − L(f) (taut at 0°) and L(f) − L(60°) (slack at 60°); the crossing is the angle at which both are equal. Goals are user-set thresholds; the defaults are 5 mm (difference between 10° and 30° in Mihata 2016), 15% (stretch of dermal allograft, Mihata 2017) and 10 mm slack at 60° (arbitrary). "Find best anchors" evaluates every pair of glenoid and humeral candidate anchors (44 × 27) for the smallest maximum length change over 0–60°; it is purely geometric and can return positions at the edge of the grid.

## Dashboard inside Planning (v0.8)
The Explore tab was merged into Planning. New panels: **What if** (superior head translation intact 3.0 mm, torn 6.4 mm, and with the selected graft where a measurement exists: fascia lata 3.0 mm, dermal allograft 4.7 mm = 3.0 + half of 3.4 mm; no value for LHBT techniques; plus thickness, subacromial pressure, length demand and clearance statements with sources) and **Literature benchmarks** (this plan against Mihata 2016 slope and 10→30° change, fixation angles of the published techniques, dermal allograft stretch, graft thickness ranges, own cadaver dimensions, head elevation, Snake tendon length, and bone clearance against AHD references). Published values are printed next to model estimates for comparison of direction and magnitude, not as validation.

## Patient geometry in the dashboard (v0.9)
When a patient case is loaded in the Patient tab (landmarks from STL or DICOM), the Planning dashboard can use the patient simulation (the shortest path over the head sphere from SG to GT, as in the Patient tab) instead of the template model for the length-change chart, key outputs, goals, plan comparison and graft size suggestion. Only single-bundle plans are supported and every technique then uses SG and GT as anchors, because the technique-specific anchors (posterosuperior glenoid rim, anterior, posterior or middle greater tuberosity) are defined on the template bones only.
## Graft size suggestion
Mediolateral length = straight SG-to-GT distance at the fixation angle + 15 mm (rule described in Ting 2023); anteroposterior width = tear length entered by the user; area = product. The patch sizes 33, 38 and 47 mm of Ting 2023 are listed for reference. LHBT techniques are not sized by this rule. The printed plan now includes the dashboard charts, goals, size suggestion, what-if panel and benchmark table.

## In silico checks (v0.10)
1. **Anchor grid.** All 1188 pairs of glenoid and humeral candidate anchors (template, glenohumeral abduction in the scapular plane, no rotation): slope of graft length between 10° and 30° from −0.40 to −0.28 mm/° (median −0.37). Only 2 pairs fall within ±0.04 of Mihata 2016 (−0.25 mm/°). The steeper slope is therefore not a matter of where the anchors are placed. Absolute lengths of 46 mm at 10° and 41 mm at 30° (±4 mm) are reproduced by 277 pairs.
2. **Wrap sphere radius.** With the default anchors the slope is −0.35 (R = 20.6 mm), −0.39 (22.9 mm, the fitted head radius), −0.44 (25.8 mm) and −0.50 mm/° (29.7 mm).
3. **Plane of elevation.** Slope −0.37 (coronal), −0.39 (30°), −0.27 (60°) and −0.08 mm/° (sagittal). Head elevation of 0–3.4 mm and rotation of ±30° change it little (head elevation does not change the slope; rotation moves it between −0.34 and −0.37).
4. **Landmark placement (four STL cases, 300 draws each, σ: SG 2, GT 3, HH 1.5, SH 3, GA and GP 2 mm).** Length at 30°: SD 3.7–4.5 mm (5–95% span about ±6–7 mm). Slope 10–30°: SD 0.04 mm/°. Taut at 0° and slack at 60° relative to 30°: SD 1.0–1.6 mm. Contribution to the length at 30° when perturbed alone: GT 2.6–3.1 mm, SG 2.1–2.2 mm, HH 0.7–2.1 mm, SH 0.9–1.2 mm, GA and GP under 0.2 mm. The dashboard has a 200-draw version with editable σ (SG, GT, HH) and a band on the length-change chart.
Conclusions: relative quantities (slope, taut, slack, ranking of plans) are robust to landmark error; absolute lengths are not and should be reported with an interval. The systematic difference from Mihata 2016 is not explained by anchor placement or landmark error; it depends on the plane of abduction and on the definition of the distance and the abduction angle in the cadaver protocol, which the app cannot reproduce without those details.

## JPEG Lossless DICOM (v0.11)
Transfer syntaxes 1.2.840.10008.1.2.4.57 and .70 (ITU T.81 process 14, single component, predictors 1–7, point transform, restart intervals) are decoded in the browser: encapsulated fragments are concatenated, Huffman tables are parsed, differences are decoded with a 9-bit lookup table and predictions are applied modulo 2^16. Checked against pydicom (with pylibjpeg) on 573 slices (46.7 million pixels) from five shoulder MRI studies (Siemens, 12-bit, 256–320 matrix): all slices identical, 1.8 s in total. Series orientation is shown as "oblique coronal" or "oblique sagittal" when the slice normal is less than 0.9 along one axis. The test images are patient data and are not part of this repository.

## Image planes and fitted template in the 3D view (v0.12)
The axial, coronal and sagittal slices at the crosshair are resampled exactly as in the 2D views and drawn as textured quads whose corners are the plane corners in patient millimetres, mapped to the scene frame (origin at the head centre; left shoulders mirrored). The template scapula is fitted to SG, GA, GP and HH (and the template glenoid centre ± 13 mm along the anteroposterior axis for GA and GP) by a similarity transform (Horn quaternion, scale from the point sets); the template humerus uses the same scale and a rotation about HH that maps the template GT and shaft directions onto the patient's GT and SH directions. On a synthetic test (scale 1.10, 20° rotation, right and left) the transform is recovered with 0.0 mm and 0.0° residual. The fitted bones carry the template's shape: they place a plausible scapula and humerus around the patient's landmarks and are not a segmentation of the patient's bones.

With a DICOM series loaded but fewer than six landmarks, the 3D view now shows the image planes (origin at HH if placed, else the volume centre) and the landmarks placed so far, hides the template model and lists the missing landmarks in an on-screen message.

## Automatic landmark suggestion on MRI/CT (v0.13)
1. **Head centre (HH):** each series is resampled to a 1.5 mm grid in patient coordinates, smoothed, and its 3D gradient computed. Voxels in the top 8% of gradient magnitude vote, along ±gradient, at radii 19–29 mm (step 2) with weight magnitude/radius; the vote volume is smoothed and its maximum is the head centre. The radius is the sphere radius with the largest mean intensity step across the surface (17–31 mm). Per patient the series are combined by the component-wise median after dropping series more than 15 mm from the median; the spread of the kept series is reported.
2. **Other landmarks (SG, GT, SH, GA, GP):** the template landmarks are scaled by the detected radius over the template head radius (22.9 mm), mirrored for a left shoulder (side from the DICOM laterality tag, or chosen by the user) and placed in patient coordinates with the template axes aligned to the scanner axes (arm at the side, scapula in its template orientation). They are starting points, not detections, and are flagged as unverified until the user places each one.
3. **Check on five shoulder MRI studies (2 left, 3 right by tag):** the head centre of the app agreed with an independent Python implementation within 0.7–4.4 mm; the kept series agreed with their median within 2.1–10.2 mm; the sign of x matched the laterality tag in all five; the circle fell on the humeral head on coronal slices in 9 of 10 single-series checks, the exception being one T1 series 20 mm off, which the median over series removes. No manual landmarks were available, so accuracy against a reference standard is unknown.

## Patient 3D view layout (v0.13.1)
Defaults reduced to avoid clutter: only the coronal image plane is shown (axial and sagittal can be switched on), plane opacity 70%, fitted template bones 50% opaque without depth writing so the image plane stays visible behind them, patient landmarks labelled by their abbreviation next to the point (full names as tooltips) instead of margin callouts, and the interaction hint disappears after the first touch or click.

## Bone shape matching on MRI (v0.14)
Candidates: the BodyParts3D template and every complete scapula+humerus STL pair loaded in the STL section (the left ones mirrored to a right-shoulder frame). Each candidate is placed by a similarity fit to the six patient landmarks (scapula to SG, GA, GP, HH; humerus rotation to GT and SH directions, same scale), then each bone is refined by pattern search over scale (0.88–1.12), rotation (±8.6°) and translation (±8 mm) about its pivot. Image score: all series are resampled to a 2 mm grid and normalised (2nd–98th percentile); for about 2500 surface points per bone the score is the mean of (intensity 3 mm outside + intensity 3 mm inside)/2 − intensity at the surface, i.e. how much darker the cortex is than its surroundings, pooled over series; the optimiser maximises that value times the square root of the fraction of points inside the volume. Candidates are ranked by the pooled score. The best match is drawn as outlines on the axial, coronal and sagittal images (scapula orange, humerus blue) and as translucent bones in 3D.
Check on five shoulder MRI studies with four CT bone pairs plus the template: runs in 6–7 s per patient; scores differ little between candidates (0.008–0.033) and the best candidate changes from patient to patient; outlines follow the humeral head well on the images checked. The landmark residual of the template is 0 by construction when the landmarks were auto-suggested from it. No segmented reference bone was available, so surface distance to the patient's real bone is unknown; this is not a segmentation.

## Correction and patient-based plan (v0.15)
**Correction.** Before v0.15 the patient simulation (Patient tab, dashboard patient mode, uncertainty estimate, STL batch table and CSV) returned the straight-line distance between SG and the simulated GT, whereas the template model used the path draped over the head sphere; earlier descriptions in this file said both were draped. From v0.15 the patient length is the shortest path over a sphere centred on HH with the measured head radius (HT landmark, the STL sphere fit, or the radius found on the images), and falls back to the straight line only if no radius is known or SG lies inside the sphere. The size suggestion still uses the straight distance, as the Ting 2023 protocol does. Numbers reported for patient cases before v0.15 are therefore straight-line values; for four STL cases the draped values at 0, 10, 30 and 60° are, for example, 45.8, 41.5, 33.1 and 21.0 mm (case 001), with a 10–30° slope of −0.42 mm/° (previously −0.38 straight-line).
**Technique anchors on the patient.** In patient mode every technique is evaluated with its template anchors (SCR.bundles, including anchors changed by the user) carried onto the patient through the template-to-patient transforms (scapula: SG, GA, GP, HH fit, humerus: GT, SH directions; or the image-refined fit after bone matching). Scapular anchors stay fixed and humeral anchors rotate with the simulated abduction exactly as the GT does; each bundle is a path over the head sphere, and bundle lengths are summed. The anchors are not located on the patient's own bone surface.
**Workflow and report.** A checklist at the top of the Patient tab shows the state of each step (images, landmarks and how many are still auto-suggested, bone shape, technique and geometry, report). The printed plan lists the geometry used, the number of verified landmarks, the bone shape and the image reliability.

## Surgical plan on the patient (v0.16)
In the Patient tab 3D view the planned graft of the Planning tab is drawn on the patient: each bundle is the path over the head sphere between the carried-over glenoid anchor and the humeral anchor rotated to the simulated abduction (flat tendon band for LHBT techniques, ribbon plus cord for fascia lata and dermal allograft), with red (glenoid) and amber (humeral) anchor dots. An isometry map can be switched on for the glenoid or humeral anchor of single-bundle plans: every candidate anchor is carried onto the patient, coloured in thirds by the largest change of patient path length over 0–60° relative to the fixation angle, and clicking a dot moves that anchor. A written surgical plan (basis, technique steps summarised from the cited papers, anchor sites with the distance moved from the default, path length per bundle, tension at 0/30/60/90°, graft size suggestion, checks and flags sorted by severity, evidence) is shown in the Patient and Planning tabs and in the printed plan.

## Patient kinematics and muscles (v0.17)
The simulated abduction (slider in the Patient tab) now also rotates the fitted humerus about the head centre with the same rotation used for the greater tuberosity (neutral-rotation offset about the shaft, then abduction about the glenoid anteroposterior axis). Template muscles can be drawn on the patient: each vertex is a blend of its scapular position (template to patient through the scapula transform; clavicle weight treated as scapular) and its humeral position (humerus transform then the simulated rotation), with the skinning weights of the template. The muscle table reports the straight distance between the centre of the scapular-side vertices (weight < 0.1) and of the humeral-side vertices (weight > 0.9) at 0, 30, 60 and 90° abduction and the percentage change from 0°. In one MRI case: supraspinatus −12, −26, −41%; infraspinatus −1, −4, −9%; subscapularis 0, +1, +4%; teres minor +9, +14, +17%. These are geometric indicators, not muscle-tendon lengths, forces or strength; the app has no muscle force model.

## Research analyses in the app (v0.18)
Tendon dimensions: per-shoulder LHB width and thickness and superior capsule thickness (n = 10 shoulders, 5 cadavers); the check column depends on the technique (Snake: width of at least 5 mm, Kim 2019; loop: tunnel as wide as the tendon); the graft-to-capsule ratio uses the shoulder's own tendon thickness for LHBT techniques and the selected graft thickness otherwise. The interval for the proportion below 5 mm is exact (Clopper-Pearson); the right-left association is an exact permutation test of Spearman's rho and the paired difference an exact Wilcoxon signed-rank test, with five pairs. Variation across patients: for each loaded CT pair the automatic (or edited) landmarks give the template-to-bone fit; the default anchors of five LHBT techniques are carried onto the bones and the path over the head sphere is measured at 0°, the chosen fixation angle and 60°; left shoulders are mirrored. Results of both analyses reproduce those computed outside the app.
